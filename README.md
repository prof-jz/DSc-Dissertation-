Enterprises face a complicated and broad cybersecurity risk and compliance landscape due to threats, maintenance, and evolving regulatory requirements.  Such enterprises are often comprised of multiple components such as traditional information technology (IT and OT) assets and services, operational technology assets, and a variety of development, test, and production environments.  Cyber-physical systems (CPS) where information and operational technologies interface and interact add nuances and potentially increases attack surface in ways that render traditional cybersecurity measures inadequate. Here I demonstrate orchestrated, human supervised artificial intelligence (AI) within digital twins (DT) of complete CPS to address these challenges.  This study addresses my key research questions (a) null hypotheses – is traditional IT endpoint detection and response (EDR) and IT security tools and practices are sufficient to secure, scan, and remediate CPS, (b) do CPS tested and secured by novel multifactor specialized security AI within a DT best ensures the suitability, feasibility, and acceptability of low-risk real-world defenses?  This work is an application of Industry 4.0 concepts where digital twinning is used extensively to model systems and projects for operational and business reasons.  Here, I use Industry 4.0 methods to create a high-fidelity DT of a CPS within a cloud environment, treating each asset as a container with the same interfaces to other assets within the twinned environment to test my research questions.  This method involves scanning, testing, and applying initial remediations on DT. AIs working within individual copies of the DT perform specialized security functions such as scanning for OS vulnerabilities, threat hunting, or validating policy compliance.  The AI feed their findings to a dashboard monitored by a human expert supported by a general-purpose machine-learning AI. The expert creates complete transparency into the automated processes of the AI, keeping compliant with OMB and NIST guidance by managing the AI.  To avoid specialized AI from impacting the scanning of the other AIs, each specialized AI may individually analyze a separate copy of the DT and report its findings to a data lake managed by an overarching general purpose large learning model (LLM) AI under human in the loop (HITL) supervision.  I extensively analyze the findings of the specialized AI to determine scope and validity of the findings when all data is considered to discover likely false positives, validating positive positives, identifying potential false negatives, assisting to risk-rank-order these findings, and preparing reporting. The human reserves and controls all remediation and change decisions.   I validate and test-functional mitigations within a DT, then run the AIs on the updated DT.  I compare the findings from the updated DT to the original DT.  Remediations found to be effective may then be recommended for deployment in the real-world enterprise.  Such an automated process may further assist cybersecurity professionals by generating Risk Management Framework (RMF) artifacts thus speeding transparent change management decisions and remediations to the real-world enterprise.  Moreover, the insights gained from AI-driven monitoring and DT Simulations can inform continuous improvements in code development practices, reducing risk and cost. This will ensure that security is embedded into every phase of the system lifecycle.  Reducing security breach risk and increasing critical system integrity shifts the enterprise stance from reactive to proactive. Proactivity is particularly relevant to the challenge of protecting systems via a strong ‘Sec’ within the CPS and DevSecOps.  My approach enables rapid identification and response to emerging threat intelligence, evolving federal policies, and novel code vulnerabilities via continuous scanning multifaceted learning specialized AIs under a general HITL LLM AI.
